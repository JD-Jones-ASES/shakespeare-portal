# Shakespeare Portal

A reading portal for Shakespeare's plays and poems, built for students who haven't yet absorbed the biblical, classical, and historical context the texts assume.

## What makes it different

Most online Shakespeare editions hand you the text and a glossary. This portal layers:

- **Archaic vocabulary** glossed in the margin (not pop-ups — the research is unkind to pop-ups)
- **Biblical allusions** with Geneva Bible 1599 citations
- **Classical and mythological** references with their source (Ovid, Plutarch, Virgil, Homer)
- **Historical and topical** context for the history plays and Elizabethan in-jokes
- **Sexual puns and wordplay** that modern readers miss
- **Rhetorical devices** named and explained when they carry the meaning

…with a **depth toggle** so advanced readers can hide everything and read the play clean.

## Status

**Live at <https://jd-jones-ases.github.io/shakespeare-portal/>** — deployed from `main` via GitHub Actions.

**Thirty-seven plays fully annotated and shipping**: *Hamlet* (356 annotations, 813-entry glossary), *A Midsummer Night's Dream* (189, 724), *Julius Caesar* (202, 1030), *Romeo and Juliet* (287, 1004), *Richard II* (262, 891), *Henry IV, Part 1* (369, 1044), *Henry IV, Part 2* (291, 1173), *Henry V* (302, 1125), *Henry VI, Part 1* (262, 1093), *Henry VI, Part 2* (307, 1051), *Henry VI, Part 3* (281, 1054), *Richard III* (364, 1000), *King John* (254, 1027), *Henry VIII* (303, 1000), *The Tempest* (247, 959), *Pericles* (248, 863), *Cymbeline* (386, 1054), *The Winter's Tale* (325, 1130), *The Taming of the Shrew* (238, 1016), *Love's Labour's Lost* (243, 1040), *The Comedy of Errors* (152, 888), *Coriolanus* (344, 1054), *Titus Andronicus* (232, 1028), *Antony and Cleopatra* (378, 996), *Macbeth* (233, 894), *King Lear* (364, 1056), *Much Ado About Nothing* (231, 922), *Twelfth Night* (231, 962), *As You Like It* (251, 1018), *The Two Gentlemen of Verona* (229, 835), *The Merchant of Venice* (254, 870), *Measure for Measure* (290, 877), *All's Well That Ends Well* (274, 981), *Troilus and Cressida* (345, 1079), *Othello* (348, 1045), *The Merry Wives of Windsor* (246, 1021), and *Timon of Athens* (253, 919) — **10,371 annotations** wired to **210 source-cited reference cards**, every claim through a three-judge fact-check. **All ten of Shakespeare's English history plays now ship**: the complete first tetralogy (the three *Henry VI* plays and *Richard III*), the Henriad (*Richard II* through *Henry V*), and the two outliers — *King John* and the late, Fletcher-co-authored *Henry VIII*. They are built around a shared "spine" of reusable historical reference cards (Edward III's sons, the king's two bodies, Holinshed's Chronicles, the great chain of being, de casibus tragedy, trial by combat, the Salic law, Agincourt) extended play by play: the Wars of the Roses set for the first tetralogy (the Temple-garden quarrel, Joan la Pucelle, Jack Cade, Towton, the Princes in the Tower, Bosworth, the Tudor myth), the Angevin succession / papal interdict / Richard the Lionheart for *King John*, and Cardinal Wolsey / the King's Great Matter / the break with Rome / Foxe's *Acts and Monuments* for *Henry VIII*. Each unusual edition needed a small, gated parser enhancement kept byte-identical for the already-shipped plays — Henry V's act-by-act Choruses, the Henry VI plays' unbracketed stage directions, *Richard III*'s inline asides, and now *Henry VIII*'s speaker-less Prologue/Epilogue, *King John*'s over-length unison speech-prefix, and *Pericles*'s per-act Gower Presenter with dumb-shows. All four **romances** of this group now ship — *The Tempest* (whose masque of the goddesses Iris, Ceres, and Juno is handled purely as character data), *Pericles* (whose narrator, the medieval poet Gower, speaks a rhymed chorus before every act), and now *Cymbeline* and *The Winter's Tale*, both of which reuse that machinery with no parser change: Cymbeline's descending god Jupiter and the family ghosts who summon him are character data like the Tempest's masque, and the Winter's Tale's Time-as-Chorus simply speaks his own scene. (Cymbeline's vendored modern text was missing its entire Welsh middle — Acts 3 and 4 — and was completed from the public-domain Moby Shakespeare edition.) Three **framing and pageant comedies** complete this round: *The Taming of the Shrew*, whose Christopher Sly Induction is rendered as a pseudo-act before Act 1 ('Induction, Scene 1', where the through-line numbering begins); *Love's Labour's Lost*, whose Nine Worthies pageant, Masque of Muscovites, and closing Spring/Winter songs are handled purely as character data; and *The Comedy of Errors*, whose two sets of identical twins are kept apart by their full speech-prefixes — none needing more than a small, gated parser touch kept byte-identical for the already-shipped plays. The three **Roman tragedies** now ship as well — *Coriolanus* (drawn from Plutarch, reusing the *Julius Caesar* Roman bank, with new cards for Menenius's fable of the belly, the tribunes of the people, and the consulship), *Titus Andronicus* (Shakespeare's most violent and most Ovidian play, whose new "Ovid bank" — Ovid's *Metamorphoses*, Senecan revenge tragedy, and the figure of the Moor on the English stage — is built to carry forward into the narrative poems, and whose racial and sexual violence is annotated with clinical, contextual care), and *Antony and Cleopatra* (the canon's most scene-heavy play — forty-two short scenes sweeping between Rome and Egypt — reusing the Roman/Plutarch bank with a new Egyptian-and-battle card set: Cleopatra as Isis, the barge on the Cydnus, Actium, the asp, the Ptolemies, and the Second Triumvirate). Coriolanus and Titus needed no parser change; *Antony and Cleopatra*, the first play with an act of more than ten scenes, required two general, byte-identical-verified parser fixes — Roman numerals above X and a combined act-and-scene header line. Finally, the two **Holinshed tragedies** round out this round, both reusing the history "spine" with no parser change: *Macbeth* (the most-taught tragedy, with new cards for the weird sisters and King James's witch-belief, the Porter's equivocation and the Gunpowder Plot, the King's Evil, and Banquo's Stuart line) and *King Lear* (built from the conflated Quarto-and-Folio text, with new cards for the devil-names Edgar's Poor Tom borrows from Samuel Harsnett, the legendary King Leir, the division of Britain, the cliff at Dover, and the eclipses). Two **festive comedies** join them — *Much Ado About Nothing* (the 'merry war' of wit between Beatrice and Benedick, the slandering of Hero, and the malapropping constable Dogberry, with new cards for the cuckold's horns, the 'nothing'/'noting' pun, the chastity double standard, the Hero-and-Leander legend, and the Elizabethan watch) and *Twelfth Night* (Viola's shipwreck and cross-dressing disguise as 'Cesario,' the gulling of the 'Puritan' steward Malvolio, and Feste's songs, with new cards for the Twelfth-Night Lord of Misrule, Puritanism, Illyria, Arion and the dolphin, Elysium, and Pythagoras's transmigration of souls). Both reuse the comedy and love banks (Cupid's arrows, Diana, Petrarch and the sonnet, the stage Machiavel, falconry and the haggard) and needed only small, gated, byte-identical-verified parser touches for their songs — attributing 'Sigh no more' to its named singer Balthasar, and reading Twelfth Night's title-case 'Song.' cue as a header. Two more comedies join them — *As You Like It* (Rosalind's cross-dressed wit in the Forest of Arden, with new cards for Ganymede, the marriage-god Hymen, the classical Golden Age, pastoral and the greenwood Robin Hood, the seven ages of man, and Renaissance melancholy) and the early *The Two Gentlemen of Verona* (the friend-versus-love plot, with new cards for the shape-changer Proteus and the humanist friendship ideal) — each needing only small, gated, byte-identical-verified parser touches for their songs and inline speech-prefixes: attributing 'Blow, blow, thou winter wind' to Amiens, the serenade 'Who is Silvia?' to the Musicians, and splitting the run-together speaker labels of the early text's comic patter. Two **problem plays** complete this round — *The Merchant of Venice* (the flesh-bond plot and the casket test, with the antisemitism of Shylock's story framed clinically through a new card on the 'Jew' on the Elizabethan stage — parallel to the existing card on the Moor — alongside new cards on usury and the bond, the three caskets, the quality of mercy, and Jacob and Laban) and *Measure for Measure* (the disguised Duke, Angelo's coercion of the novice Isabella, and the bed-trick, with new cards on the disguised ruler, the 'precise' Puritan, the bawdy-houses of Vienna, the bed-trick, and the Sermon-on-the-Mount source of the title). Both reuse forward-seeded cards (the friendship ideal for Antonio and Bassanio, the friar's confession for the disguised Duke, the Moor for the Prince of Morocco) and needed only small, gated, byte-identical-verified parser touches: routing Merchant's unattributed casket-song to the Musicians, and reading Measure's joint 'Angelo and Escalus' cue as a single shared line. Their difficult material — Shylock's forced conversion and Angelo's sexual blackmail — is annotated with clinical, contextual care. Two more **problem plays** close this round — *All's Well That Ends Well* (Helena, the 'clever wench' who cures the King and wins the reluctant Bertram by the bed-trick, with new cards on the Boccaccio/Painter source, the King's fistula and the woman healer, and Parolles the braggart *miles gloriosus*) and *Troilus and Cressida* (a bitter, disillusioned anatomy of the Trojan War and the lovers' betrayal, with a six-card delta on the matter of Troy, the deflated Greek and Trojan heroes, Achilles and Patroclus, the 'false Cressid' tradition, Pandarus and the word 'pander,' and the Judgement of Paris). All's Well needed no parser change; Troilus needed a slug-gated fix for its armed Prologue plus a general fix for an edition that reprints the act header before every scene. Their hardest material — All's Well's coerced marriage and bed-trick, Troilus's misogyny toward Cressida and the dishonourable killing of Hector — is presented critically and without endorsement. Finally, the **mop-up trio** completes the dramatic canon — *Othello* (the much-taught tragedy of jealousy and the manipulator Iago, with new cards on the strawberry handkerchief and 'ocular proof,' the Cyprus/Ottoman war, Iago's 'green-eyed monster,' and the Pontic sea, and the racism toward Othello framed clinically through the existing card on the Moor on the English stage), *The Merry Wives of Windsor* (Falstaff's three gullings by the merry wives, with new cards on Herne the Hunter and the Latin-grammar lesson, and the Welsh and French dialect of Evans and Caius handled through glossary guidance), and *Timon of Athens* (the lavish lord turned misanthrope, co-written with Thomas Middleton, with new cards on the classical Timon-the-misanthrope tradition from Lucian, Alcibiades and ungrateful Athens, and the mock-banquet of stones). None needed a parser change — Timon's sprawling minor cast of apostrophe-labelled servants and unison cues is handled entirely as character data. **With these three, all of Shakespeare's plays now ship and Phase A is complete; the 6 remaining catalog works are the poems and Sonnets (Phase B).**

The content pipeline (ingest → annotate → fact-check → validate → build) is proven and repeatable. To build another play, an agent follows [docs/BUILD_A_PLAY.md](docs/BUILD_A_PLAY.md) — the step-by-step runbook.

## Run locally

```
cd site
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open `http://localhost:4321`.

To validate data against schemas:

```
cd scripts
npm install
npm run validate
```
